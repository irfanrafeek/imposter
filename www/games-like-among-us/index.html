3 Free Games Like Among Us You Can Play in a Browser

Someone suggests Among Us. Half the group has it, half doesn’t, and suddenly there is a download, an app store password nobody remembers and a ten minute wait between you and playing anything at all. By the time everyone is in, the moment has gone a bit flat.

The short version: there are three free social deduction games at impostorgames.com that run in any phone browser. Nothing to install, no account to make, 3 to 20 players. One person creates a room, shares a 4-character code or a QR code, and everyone else is in from their own phone. First round starts in about a minute.

What Among Us actually asks of you

Among Us is a very good game and this page is not going to pretend otherwise. It has been running since 2018, it invented the vocabulary half of us now use for this whole genre, and if your group already has it installed you should probably just play that.

But it is a video game, and video games come with setup. Everyone needs the app, on the same version, and everyone needs to get through the update prompt first. It is free on phones and paid on PC and consoles, so “free” depends on what your friends are holding. It is built for 4 to 15 players, which is a problem at both ends if you are three people or seventeen. And a round is a proper sit-down session rather than something you squeeze in before the food arrives.

None of that matters when your group is already set up. All of it matters when you are standing in someone’s kitchen with six people, six different phones and about twenty minutes.

What you give up

Worth saying plainly, because a comparison page that only lists its own strengths is not much use to anyone.

  • There is no spaceship. No map, no tasks, no vents, no little bean characters.
  • There is no progression, no cosmetics, nothing to unlock.
  • These are built for people who can see or hear each other. They work fine over a video call, but they are not designed for playing with strangers on the internet.

What survives is the part most people are actually there for: one person is lying, everyone is watching everyone else, and the accusations start about ninety seconds in.

Which of the three to play

They are all the same shape underneath. Everyone gets something except one person, who gets something slightly different and has to bluff their way through. What changes is what your group has to work with.

Word The safest first pick. Everyone gets the same secret word, the impostor gets a vague hint, and you go round the circle saying one word each. Needs nothing but phones. Draw Everyone draws the same word on one shared canvas, one turn at a time, each in their own colour. The impostor has to fake a line for a picture they cannot identify yet. Dance The loud one, and the one people film. Everyone hears the same song through their headphones except the impostor, who gets a different track. Everyone needs headphones for this one.

The other names people use for this

Among Us is the reference point most people reach for, but it is not the only one, and the game your group is half-remembering might be one of these instead.

  • Spyfall. Everyone knows the location except the spy, who has to work it out from what people say. Closest to our word game.
  • Fake Artist Goes to New York. The drawing one. Everyone draws the same thing except the fake artist. That is our draw game almost exactly.
  • Undercover, sometimes sold as Werewords or Insider. Another name for the word version.
  • Werewolf and Mafia. The ancestors of all of it. Bigger groups, a moderator, and people getting eliminated round by round. We do not have this one.

The umbrella term, if you want the one that turns up in reviews, is social deduction. One player has secret information and a reason to lie about it, and the rest of the group has to work out who.

Common questions

Is there an official browser version of Among Us?

No. Innersloth publishes it as a download on every platform it supports, so if playing in a browser is the requirement, you need a different game. That is what this page is for.

What is the best game like Among Us for 6 players?

Six is a good number for all three of ours. If it is your first time, start with the word game: it needs nothing but phones, the rules explain themselves in about a sentence, and nobody has to find headphones first.

Do we all need to be in the same room?

Not strictly, but it helps. These are built around people talking to each other, so a room or a video call both work. Playing silently with strangers is not really what they are for.

Is it actually free, or free with something attached?

Actually free. No sign-up, no download, no ads, no paid tier. The site does not use cookies or collect personal data.

How long does a round take?

A few minutes. Most groups play several in a row rather than stopping after one, which is the main practical difference from a full session of Among Us.

How do people join?

One person creates the room and gets a 4-character code and a QR code. Everyone else opens the site on their own phone and types the code in, or scans. Nothing gets installed on anyone’s phone.
