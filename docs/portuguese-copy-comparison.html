Every user-facing string in the Brazilian Portuguese build, next to the English it sits beside. 973 rows across the hub, the three games and the shared block. The Portuguese has not shipped yet and no native speaker has read it, so treat this as a draft rather than a record.

Read the gaps as decisions, not as bugs

Three kinds of row will not line up, and all three are deliberate.

  • The FAQ lists differ on purpose. Five questions are the core set every language asks: what the game is, how many players, whether it is free, whether there is an app, and whether you can play on one phone. Everything else is a search question, and people search differently in each language. English asks where to play the Imposter Dance Challenge; Portuguese asks whether there is something like Among Us with nothing to install, and whether you can play over a video call. Rows marked English only or Portuguese only are that, not an omission.
  • The song pools are different music. English offers eleven categories. Portuguese offers four, chosen for Brazil, and they are not a translation of any English four.
  • Some English sections have no Portuguese at all. The guide pages and the “More to read” links exist only in English, so they are left out of this document entirely rather than shown as empty rows.

Rows highlighted in yellow are byte-identical once tags and entities are stripped. Most are proper nouns or single words that are genuinely the same in both languages. A few are worth a second look.

What the Portuguese was written against

  • Brazilian, not neutral. Unlike the Spanish, which was written to sit comfortably in both Latin America and Spain, the Portuguese is written for Brazil. It uses você, celular, fone de ouvido and a galera without hedging.
  • Written in Portuguese, not translated into it. The English is the reference for meaning, never for sentence shape. Where a row reads like the English with Portuguese words in it, that is the defect worth reporting.
  • It is a party game. Copy that is correct but stiff is still wrong.
  • Lowercase openings are deliberate on the discussion screen. The subtitle and the waiting line run lowercase against sentence case for the host’s instruction, in all three languages.
